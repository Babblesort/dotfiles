# The Quiet Giant

Beneath the cedar, moss, and fern,
where mountain rivers twist and turn,
a shadow moves on silent feet,
too large for trails, too shy to meet.

The hikers find a footprint deep,
pressed in the mud where willows weep;
they measure it with shaking hands,
and wonder who among them stands.

A blurry photo, grainy, gray,
a shape that turns and slips away;
the forest hums, the ravens call,
and Bigfoot isn't there at all.

He doesn't want your fame or flash,
your campfire songs, your cooler stash;
he wants the dusk, the rain, the pine,
the hush of dawn, the old tree line.

So if you walk where no one goes,
and hear a branch snap, soft and slow,
just nod politely to the trees —
he's watching, and he's glad you're pleased.
